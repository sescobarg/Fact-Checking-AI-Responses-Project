# Source Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines what counts as acceptable evidence for fact-checking AI-generated responses. The goal is to support consistent, transparent, and professional human evaluation.

## Preferred Sources

Use the most authoritative source available for the claim:

- Official organization, government, institutional, or product documentation.
- Academic publications, reputable research institutions, or peer-reviewed sources.
- Established reference sources for stable facts.
- Reputable news organizations for current events, with attention to publication date.
- Domain-specific authorities for health, law, finance, science, safety, or public policy.

## Sources to Avoid

Avoid using these as primary evidence:

- AI-generated answers or chatbot outputs.
- Unsourced blogs, forums, or social media posts.
- Content farms, copied summaries, or pages with unclear authorship.
- Outdated pages for time-sensitive claims.
- Sources that only discuss a related topic but do not support the exact claim.

## Time-Sensitive Claims

For claims about current leaders, prices, laws, policies, product features, releases, statistics, or events:

- Prefer the most recent reliable source.
- Check publication or update dates.
- Mark the claim as `outdated` if it was once correct but is no longer accurate.
- Mention timing limits in `evidence_summary` when the date matters.

## Sensitive Domains

For health, legal, financial, safety, or security claims:

- Prefer official or expert sources.
- Use higher severity when an incorrect or unsupported claim could cause harm.
- Avoid presenting the evaluation as professional advice.
- Use cautious wording when evidence is limited or context-dependent.

## Evidence Standard

A source should be:

- Relevant to the exact claim.
- Credible for the domain.
- Current enough for the topic.
- Specific enough to justify the verdict.
- Traceable through a stable URL when possible.

If evidence is weak, indirect, missing, or ambiguous, do not mark the claim as `supported`.
